# EVE — ART BIBLE v1.0

**Status:** Canonical Visual Direction  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Authority:** All production artwork should follow this document unless a later approved art-bible version supersedes it.

## GLOBAL CINEMATIC VISUAL GRAMMAR

**HOLD ON DIALOGUE. CUT ON ACTION.** These rules apply to all current and future EVE chapters, governing both prose and visual production.

The owner's complete [Global Cinematic Art Rules](GLOBAL_CINEMATIC_ART_RULES.md) are authoritative for all current and future chapters. Use the [scene visual plan template](SCENE_VISUAL_PLAN_TEMPLATE.md) while authoring and the [prose/art validation checklist](ART_REVIEW_CHECKLIST.md#proseart-authoring-review) before production. The rules' examples illustrate visual grammar; they do not establish new events, relationships, objects or character identities in EVE canon.

- Hold the current art while characters talk in the same physical arrangement. Speaker changes, thoughts, emotional shifts, pauses, choices, and ordinary micro-gestures do not require a new shot.
- Create a new shot for meaningful movement, physical character entry, a new significant communication participant, important object action, location change, or meaningful change in spatial relationships.
- Show only actions that have occurred. Art follows completed player action and established state; it must never anticipate a choice or imply an unchosen outcome.
- Meaningful movement in prose creates an art commitment. Preserve environment, wardrobe, character, and object continuity between shots.
- Intimate scenes follow the same visual grammar and existing state/consent rules; artwork cannot establish consent or relationship meaning.
- Assign stable shot IDs such as `c05.s06.shot01` and maintain a visual beat map for every scene. See [Cinematic Shot Production](#cinematic-shot-production) below.

## VISUAL THESIS

EVE is a **dark adult espionage graphic novel** presented through polished 2D animation-style illustration.

The visual language combines:

**prestige graphic novel + sophisticated adult animation + classic spy-poster elegance + modern cinematic noir**

The art should feel:

**Elegant. Dangerous. Adult. Seductive. Controlled. Expensive. Psychological.**

EVE must NOT look like photorealistic AI art, generic anime, a dating simulator, a comedy cartoon, or a superhero comic.

The world is glamorous on the surface and dangerous underneath.

# CORE STYLE

## Rendering

- polished 2D illustration
- clean, confident linework
- semi-cel shading
- strong graphic shadows
- controlled highlights
- crisp silhouettes
- cinematic depth
- selective environmental detail
- subtle texture rather than photographic rendering

Characters should appear illustrated, not rendered as photographs.

Skin should use graphic shading and controlled highlights rather than pores or hyperrealistic texture.

## Character proportions

Adult characters use elegant, believable anatomy with slight fashion-illustration exaggeration.

Favor:

- long clean lines
- strong silhouettes
- elegant posture
- defined shoulders
- expressive hands
- distinctive profiles
- controlled physical confidence

Avoid caricature or extreme body exaggeration unless intentionally character-specific.

# CINEMATIC SHOT PRODUCTION

## Hold dialogue; cut on meaningful visual change

When the same participants remain in the same physical arrangement and simply speak, keep one image. Do not alternate portraits or reaction shots merely because the speaker, emotional tone, question, thought, pause, or choice menu changes. Camera framing options above describe how to compose a required shot, not reasons to interrupt a held dialogue image.

A new shot is required when the prose establishes:

- **Meaningful movement:** standing, sitting, crossing a room, approaching, departing, entering a vehicle, or another action that changes the visible situation.
- **Physical entry:** a character joins the scene. Show the updated composition and actual participants.
- **Communication entry:** a new significant participant joins an active call or other communication. Represent the established communication medium without implying that a remote participant is physically present or visually revealing an unknown identity.
- **Important object action:** a narratively significant object is opened, handed over, picked up, put away, or otherwise acted upon. Depict its actual resulting state and custody.
- **Location transition:** every physical location change requires its own appropriate visual, even when dialogue continues. Do not invent intermediate locations the prose never establishes.
- **Material time transition:** use a canonical environment variant when elapsed time changes lighting or surroundings. Preserve geography; change weather, temporary props or wardrobe only when established.
- **Meaningful spatial change:** proximity, separation, blocking, contact, or relative positions change in a way that matters to the scene.

The shot must depict the described action or its established result, not a generic portrait. Several descriptions of one continuous visual beat may share a shot when it faithfully represents that beat; distinct meaningful changes must not be hidden inside a dialogue hold.

Micro-gestures normally stay within the held shot: a blink, small smile, glance, breath, or minor hand adjustment does not require a cut. If such a gesture becomes an important object action, establishes contact, or meaningfully changes the composition, treat it as a visual beat.

## Prose creates an art commitment

If prose describes meaningful visible movement, production must account for it in the visual beat map and provide the corresponding shot. Do not casually add walking, sitting, approaching, or object handling as dialogue filler. During writing, choose movement deliberately and synchronize the prose beat with its art requirement.

Missing art is a production gap, not permission to silently remove or rewrite approved story action. These rules govern presentation and production; they do not change story canon, character identity, or gameplay state.

## Player action and branch timing

Keep the current valid image while the player considers a choice. Switch to an action's shot only after the player has chosen it, the action has completed, and the resulting state supports the image. Never show an offered action, destination, object transfer, touch, or intimacy as though it has already happened.

For each branch, record the conditions that allow its shot and what must already have occurred. Refusal, delay, cancellation, and alternate actions must retain or receive art consistent with their own outcomes. A shared image is valid only when its visible contents are true for every branch that uses it. If no valid asset is available, record the gap; never substitute an image that invents an outcome.

## Continuity between shots

### Locked environment rule

An approved locked recurring environment master is immutable. Character movement,
entry/exit, wardrobe changes and prop actions do not authorize a generator to redraw
the room. Prefer deterministic compositing of transparent character, prop and
object-state layers over full-scene regeneration. Generation supplies missing layers;
it does not recreate locked architecture. Preserve background pixels outside layer
coverage, record source hashes and explicit scale/anchor/z-order metadata, and review
physical fit against the camera's floor/table/chair/door/mirror calibration. Never
resize furniture to accommodate a character. Any distinct lighting master requires
separate explicit approval. See the [global rule](GLOBAL_CINEMATIC_ART_RULES.md) and
[offline compositor](../../tools/visual/compositor/README.md). These production
records neither change save schema nor establish story or identity state.

Carry forward established environment layout, entrances, furniture, lighting/time, weather, character positions, face, hair, age, body proportions, wardrobe, accessories, injuries/marks and object placement/custody. Change them only when supported by the scene or an established transition. A camera change must not invent a wardrobe change, relocate a prop, reverse an object handoff, or add an absent participant. Movement inside the locked Adrian/Evelynn apartment never authorizes redesigning it; the same rule applies to every locked recurring environment.

Use approved character and environment references. Artwork depicts established state; it does not establish biography, identity acceptance, knowledge, attraction, or relationship status.

## Intimate scenes

Intimate scenes use the same hold/cut rules, branch timing, and continuity checks. Every shot must respect the existing adult eligibility, scene access, state, and consent requirements. Attraction, willingness, consent, instrumental motive, and relationship meaning remain distinct; an image must not infer one from another.

Never anticipate acceptance, escalation, contact, or a wardrobe change before the relevant chosen action is complete. Declining, stopping, or withdrawing consent must not display an image implying continuation. Visual intensity does not authorize a new story event or bypass the existing presentation rules.

AdultSceneSpec remains authoritative. Never depict sex or nudity before the authorized canonical state, intimacy after withdrawal, coercion in a voluntary encounter, or voluntary desire inferred from compliance. A stop/separation needs the changed-distance shot supported by the actual action.

## Stable shot IDs and per-scene visual beat maps

Assign each distinct required composition a stable ID using chapter, scene, and shot components, for example `c05.s06.shot01`. This is a naming example, not a new canonical Chapter 5 event. Keep IDs stable through prose edits, asset revisions, and production reordering; do not renumber existing shots or reuse a retired ID for a different beat. Give incompatible branch compositions distinct IDs and record their conditions explicitly. Display order belongs in the beat map, not in assumptions about sequential numbering.

Maintain a visual beat map for every scene, including its opening shot and dialogue holds. Each entry records:

| Field | Required production information |
| --- | --- |
| Beat and prose anchor | Where the visible event occurs and its order within the applicable branch. |
| Shot ID / hold | Stable shot ID for a new composition, or the existing shot ID held during dialogue. |
| Trigger and depiction | Why a cut is required and the exact action or established result to show. |
| State and branch conditions | Required participants, completed player action, eligibility/consent where applicable, and outcomes that must not use this shot. |
| Composition and continuity | Location, physical/remote participants, positions, wardrobe, lighting, important objects, and continuity changes from the preceding valid shot. |
| Asset and review status | Linked art specification/reference, asset revision when available, approval status, and any outstanding production gap. |

Workflow: author the scene, map its meaningful visual beats, assign stable shot IDs, prepare each shot's art specification, and review the resulting assets against prose, state, and continuity. Dialogue between visual beats holds the current image. A beat-map entry or shot ID is a production requirement, not asset approval or an automatic runtime binding.

Before marking a scene visually complete, check every supported branch: all meaningful movements have shots, dialogue holds remain stable, cuts occur only after their triggering events, no image anticipates a choice, and continuity and consent remain accurate.

Each asset specification must declare scene/beat, location/time, physically present and remote participants, communication medium, wardrobe/presentation, positions, object/evidence custody, relationship/intimacy limits, completed prerequisite action and prohibited conflicting states. Location alone never makes an asset valid on another branch. Use the eight named review flags in the checklist; a written plan is not proof of runtime enforcement.

Production lifecycle: **SPEC → STAGING → REVIEW → PASS / REVISE / REJECT → HUMAN APPROVAL → PRODUCTION**. PASS alone is neither production authorization nor canonical-reference approval. A scene reference cannot promote an asset. For already-authored or frozen chapters, record visual gaps and plan compatible presentation work; do not rewrite authenticated story history to reduce the art count.

# ADULT TONE

EVE is an adult story.

Sensuality should feel integrated into character, power, relationships and espionage rather than pasted onto the story.

Visual presentation can range from restrained to mature depending on the scene, but the underlying art direction remains:

**cinematic, sophisticated, psychologically grounded and character-driven.**

The same characters must remain recognizable regardless of presentation intensity.

# STYLE CONSISTENCY RULE

Every generated or commissioned EVE image should pass this test:

> Could this image plausibly be a frame from the same sophisticated animated espionage series as the canonical Evelynn Helix gala image?

If not, reject or revise it.

# PRODUCTION PRIORITY

Consistency is more important than individual-image perfection.

For important characters establish:

1. canonical face
2. front view
3. 3/4 view
4. profile
5. full-body proportions
6. expression sheet
7. canonical wardrobe
8. alternate wardrobe
9. lighting references
10. approved character assets

Only after the design is stable should dedicated character-generation training or LoRA-style adaptation be considered.
