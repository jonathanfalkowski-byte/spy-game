# Maya and Evelynn: visual identity gate

Maya Reyes and Evelynn Vale must read as different people even when both wear dark clothes, share a location, or appear without a caption. A wardrobe change, lighting change, or scene label cannot carry the distinction alone. This is a review gate for new art, not a new story or character-canon decision.

| Character | Source to compare | Stable visual cues to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maya | `art/production/opening/axiom-opening-office-shot01-maya-v3-transparent-production.png`; `art/staging/cast-scenes/eve-cast-maya-v1.png` | Warm brown skin, watchful dark eyes, black hair commonly gathered in a loose high knot, softer and more compact facial silhouette. Her established office look is a navy suit with turned-back cuffs. An off-duty hairstyle may change, but her face must remain Maya's. |
| Evelynn | `art/reference/evelynn/evelynn-canon-front-v1.png`; `art/reference/evelynn/evelynn-canon-three-quarter-v1.png`; `art/production/opening/clinic-recovery-mirror-look-v1-production.png` | Distinct angular facial planes and cheekbones, pronounced brows, almond eyes, dark brunette hair and its established hairline. Clothing and hairstyle vary by chosen presentation; the face must remain recognizably tied to the player character, not Maya. |

Sloane's white hair streak is not a marker for either woman. Do not copy Evelynn's gala earrings, face, hairline, or eye shape into Maya art. Do not use `art/staging/cast-scenes/eve-cast-maya-pilot-v1.png` as a reference: its existing review rejects it for Evelynn identity bleed.

Before promoting a new Maya or Evelynn shot, compare the candidate with both characters' sources at full size and at the game's desktop and mobile display sizes. Check the face with clothes and scene context mentally removed: facial structure, eyes, brows, nose, hairline, hair color, and silhouette should still identify the correct woman. If the identity depends on a navy blazer, coffee cup, location, or caption, keep the candidate in staging for correction. Use each character's own source image as the identity reference; the other character may be used only as a negative comparison, not as conditioning.
