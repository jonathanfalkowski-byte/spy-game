# EVE — Home in Another Skin

- **Status:** Implemented story treatment; new-run content version 12; content 11 retained for legacy replay
- **Scope:** Post-Stage One apartment sequence before Glass House arrival
- **Placement:** Sublevel 17 departure → optional apartment reset or direct transport → Glass House arrival
- **Target length:** Approximately 10–15 minutes, depending on optional inspection and contact

## Purpose

This is Evelynn Vale’s first return to Adrian’s apartment after Stage One. It is a separate visit from the opening apartment, the pre-clinic evening, and Chapter 3’s later Home After Glass House scene.

The clinic framed the change through Voss, Sloane, equipment and operational language. The apartment briefly removes those witnesses. The scene asks what the changed life feels like when nobody else is in the room, then leaves the answer to the player.

The sequence observes embodiment and administrative intrusion. It does not declare that Adrian has accepted or rejected Evelynn, derive sexuality or corruption from one action, or turn private reflection into NPC knowledge.

## Continuity and entry

The existing accepted clinic-departure route provides a short reset window before transport to the Glass House. Sloane remains at Axiom. Evelynn leaves wearing the clinic-confirmed outfit; an optional home stop offers a delivered case of alternate presentations before the invitation time. This is a practical interval, not an unexplained time skip.

The entry state carries forward:

- Stage One’s actual physical progress, voice and face result;
- the selected Executive, Socialite or Shadow presentation and any authored customization;
- phone monitoring, restricted badge status, employment and housing consequences;
- only available clinic belongings: monitored phone, restricted badge, keys and invitation; Glass House capture has not happened;
- Maya’s prior invitation, morning response and any earned disclosures;
- Sloane’s existing knowledge and the source of each known fact;
- private interpretations from the clinic, which remain private.

No new mission, medical stage, relationship score or identity verdict is created here. The sequence must not silently restore terminated employment, change a housing deadline or make the phone private.

## Scene flow

### 1. The reset window

Open in the vehicle or residential lift after Sublevel 17. Sloane offers the residential stop; direct transport remains available and waits away from the arrival queue. The driver or building attendant completes the physical transition; the player is never teleported between locations.

The city is ordinary outside the glass. The apartment tower remains the same landmark from the opening. The contrast is physical: the same seat, stairs and building now meet a body with a different balance and voice.

The player may review the operation brief, the invitation credentials and existing messages. Review is read-only. It cannot create a new Maya arrangement or reveal a hidden sender.

### 2. A familiar door with a changed response

The residential system accepts the restricted badge and acknowledges the current display identity with an established player-facing form such as **WELCOME, EVELYNN VALE**. The exact administrative wording should be checked against the existing account architecture before becoming canon.

If the system presents an Adrian record, it must use only an existing status and source. A suspended or restricted record may be shown only if the current state already supports it. Otherwise the scene shows the system recognizing Evelynn’s credential without inventing a contradictory Adrian status.

The effect is clear without explanation: the infrastructure around Adrian’s home has begun adapting to Evelynn while the physical rooms remain unchanged.

### 3. Small ordinary inspections

Use a small set of strong callbacks rather than a catalogue. Each inspection is optional, appears immediately, and records once. Rereading never creates a second reward or conversation.

| Inspection | What the player can observe | What remains unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mirror | The face, posture, voice and familiar room occupy the same frame. The player may look closely, glance away or test an expression. | No acceptance, rejection, sexuality or corruption is inferred. |
| Jacket / shirt | The shirt is where Adrian left it; the shoulders and fall of the fabric are different now. The old jacket can be held, tried or set down. | The clothing remains physically present. It does not prove that a former identity is gone. |
| Bathroom routine | A familiar sequence takes a little more attention because balance, reach and voice are unfamiliar. | The scene does not become anatomical description or a medical quiz. |
| Personal object | A watch, glass, photograph or work item provides continuity with the opening. The player may keep it nearby or leave it in place. | The object does not determine what Evelynn feels. |

The player can also note the medical package residue or restricted badge, but only one or two environmental details should receive full prose. The rest stay in the journal as concise observations.

### 4. Axiom’s delivery

Appropriate Glass House clothing is already in the apartment or arrives through an established delivery. The fact of delivery is the intrusion: Axiom entered or arranged access while Evelynn was in Sublevel 17. Do not invent a new security breach or claim the player knows who physically entered.

The established loadouts remain:

- **Executive:** authority and professional clarity;
- **Socialite:** warmth, visibility and social access;
- **Shadow:** unobtrusive movement and lower immediate attention.

The player may inspect each outfit, compare it with the body and choose one. This choice is partly operational and partly personal because Sloane is not standing over the selection. The scene records the choice and its reason only if the player selects an authored reflection; it does not score identity acceptance.

### 5. The apartment mirror

The mirror is the emotional center, but it remains player-controlled. Available actions should include:

- inspect technically;
- look briefly, then turn away;
- repeat a familiar morning or evening routine;
- test an expression or the new voice;
- adjust one voluntary detail;
- stand silently and leave the feeling unnamed.

Each action receives a concrete physical response. None converts a private thought into dialogue. An action can be recalled later as “you tested the voice” or “you left the mirror quickly,” but no character may claim to know why unless the player says so.

### 6. One choice nobody required

Offer one modest presentation decision outside mission necessity: an old watch, a piece of jewellery, a hair adjustment, a cosmetics detail, a fragrance or an object carried from Adrian’s prior life. The player can add it, omit it or leave the choice unresolved.

The prose should show the immediate sensory or practical result. It must not moralize the choice, equate it with femininity or identity acceptance, or award a hidden alignment result. It is evidence of authorship over one detail under constrained conditions.

### 7. Maya contact

Contact is conditional on the existing Maya state. Do not create a new check-in merely to fill the scene.

- The existing 06:30 check-in is before the clinic appointment. By this 18:02 apartment visit it has already been answered or missed; do not schedule a second call. Offer a review of the existing thread, a bounded update, or no contact.
- If no arrangement exists, offer a short departure message or no contact. Its wording uses only facts Maya already knows.
- A new identity disclosure is available only as explicit speech or text. A changed voice alone does not teach Maya the binding between Adrian and Evelynn.
- The player can answer, text instead, postpone, partially disclose, warn her that something has changed, or avoid contact when those actions are authored by the existing state.

Show the monitoring scope before sending. A monitored phone is not a secure channel, and availability to Axiom is not proof that Sloane personally read the exchange. Maya’s received information, response and belief are separate records.

### 8. Leaving for the Glass House

The exit completes the practical sequence: collect phone, badge, invitation and selected presentation; pass through the residential controls; enter transport. The player sees the old apartment still physically intact while the administrative and bodily context has changed.

The transition goes directly to the existing Glass House car/arrival scene. Do not repeat the mission brief, the transformation summary or the clinic goodbye. The Glass House begins with arrival and presentation payoff.

## Information and consequence rules

- Environmental observations are facts attributed to the player’s inspection.
- Sloane’s monitoring explanation is her claim, not proof of unlimited surveillance.
- Maya’s knowledge contains only delivered words and independently sourced prior facts.
- Private mirror thoughts remain private.
- Presentation choice, mirror action and voluntary accessory are distinct from forced physical adaptation.
- The pre-gala inspection cannot reference future captures. After Glass House, Chapter 3 separately preserves custody: Sloane owns audio, the monitored phone holds any retained photograph, and the token remains a separate physical asset.
- A missed contact is a missed contact. It does not become a hidden emotional judgment or a new appointment.

## Estimated duration and interaction density

An engaged route should take about 10–15 minutes:

- 2–3 minutes for arrival and administrative re-entry;
- 3–5 minutes for two or three optional apartment inspections;
- 2–3 minutes for mirror and voluntary presentation choice;
- 2–4 minutes for an eligible Maya exchange or deliberate noncontact;
- under one minute for the completed departure.

The scene contains meaningful interpretation, embodiment and disclosure choices. Navigational clicks should be brief. Do not add a second wardrobe panel, a personality questionnaire or repeated mirror prompts merely to lengthen the sequence.

## Chapter 3 continuity implications

Chapter 3’s Home After Glass House scene must remain a separate later visit. It begins after Evelynn has operated publicly as Evelynn, so its apartment callbacks can refer to the prior home sequence without repeating first-contact discovery.

The later scene may ask what happened to the chosen outfit, accessory, message or apartment observation, but only when the player actually made that choice. It should not assume that selecting presentation at home meant identity acceptance, that Maya learned a disclosure, or that Axiom’s delivery proves who controls the apartment.

The new sequence also means Chapter 3 Scene 1 should begin with an already established homeward return and avoid reintroducing the apartment as if it were Evelynn’s first time there. Its Sloane reporting exchange can cite the earlier residential entry as a prior event, not duplicate it.

## Items deliberately rejected as padding

- a second mandatory transformation explanation;
- a full clothing inventory with no changed interaction;
- repeated mirror choices that only restate the same uncertainty;
- a new emergency message that forces Maya into the plot;
- a hidden identity or morality score derived from ordinary presentation;
- a long administrative audit that does not change what the player can do;
- any new medical mechanism, later stage or mystery revelation.
