# Endgame & reconvergence — the shared operation (design draft)

**Design draft for owner review.** No runtime, chapters, state or art are implemented by this
document. It designs the point of the diamond: where all four route lanes reconverge, what
truth resolves, how Sloane's motive lands, and **who the board member is**. It is the gate for
**Chapter 9 ("Assembling the Case")** and every lane's landing. Builds on
[CAMPAIGN_ROUTE_MAP.md](CAMPAIGN_ROUTE_MAP.md) (the diamond, reconvergence doctrine),
[CHAPTER_6_PROOF_AND_COUNTERPOWER.md](CHAPTER_6_PROOF_AND_COUNTERPOWER.md) (ORACLE / the ledger
leaf / Celeste-Marcus scope), the own-power scripts
([Ch7](scripts/CHAPTER_7_OWN_POWER_SCRIPT.md), [Ch8](scripts/CHAPTER_8_OWN_POWER_SCRIPT.md)),
and the [Master GDD](../design/EVE_MASTER_GDD.md) autonomy law.

---

## 0. What the player already knows walking in (canon recap)

By the end of the own-power route (and the equivalent close of every lane) the truth ladder is:

- The protagonist is **Adrian Vale — Candidate 7A** of **Project Eve**, an *adaptive identity
  program*. The "Evelynn/Evelyn" they wear is not invented; it is the **reused legend of a real
  prior operative** ("the Singapore Evelyn") whose whole life was pulled off a shelf and fitted
  to Adrian. (`chapter3` dossier: "VALE, ADRIAN — Candidate 7A; identity package ready";
  "PROJECT EVE — adaptive identity program".)
- **ORACLE** (the predictive system, clearance denied to Adrian) predicted Adrian would adopt
  the identity **willingly** and that **Sloane's control would be low** — and the operation
  **proceeded anyway**. (`c6.oracle-seen`.)
- **Meridian Holdings** is not Helix and not Axiom. It is a **private intelligence concern that
  manufactures operations — identities, legends, whole people — and sells them.** **Project Eve
  is a product. Axiom is a client. Sloane is a client's officer.** (`c8.meridian = product`.)
- The reuse of the prior Evelyn's legend was **authorized at Meridian board level**, above
  Sloane. One name on that board is **someone Adrian has already met, and did not expect.**
  (Ch8 close: "a board above Sloane, one name on it you are almost sure you have met.")

**What is still unresolved and belongs to the endgame** (deferred from Ch6, per the route map):
the **board member's name**, **Sloane's actual motive**, the **why** of reusing this specific
legend, and **what Adrian can now do about any of it**. This document resolves all four.

---

## 1. The truth that resolves (endgame spoiler bible)

The single operation all lanes converge on is the answer to **"who signed off on reusing her,
and why."** The shape:

1. **Meridian sells continuity, not identities.** The valuable product is not a fake person from
   scratch — it is a **legend with real history already lived into it**: verifiable travel,
   real people who remember "her," an evidentiary footprint no fabricator can match. A *used*
   legend is worth more than a new one. The prior Evelyn was **decommissioned** (retired, dead,
   or burned — see §2 decision), and her lived-in legend became **inventory**. Adrian is the
   buyer's fitting.
2. **The Singapore Evelyn was a person, and someone on the board knew her.** The reuse was not
   authorized by a stranger. It was signed by a board member **who had personally known the
   original** — which is exactly why they knew the legend was good, and exactly what makes the
   signature monstrous: they spent a person they had touched. (This is the hook that makes the
   "face you've met" reveal land — see §3.)
3. **Sloane is inside the same trap she set.** Sloane is Axiom's officer on a Meridian contract.
   She did not author the reuse; she executed a product decision made over her head, and the
   ORACLE prediction (that she could not truly hold Adrian) was **known to Meridian before she
   was handed the file.** She was set up to run an operation the vendor's own system said would
   slip — because a slipping asset that stays useful is a **better long-run product** than a
   controlled one. Sloane's leash was always partly around her own neck. (This is her motive
   resolution — §4.)
4. **ORACLE is the lever, on every route.** The operation was sold to Axiom (and to Sloane's
   superiors) on a controllability premise that ORACLE had already scored **false**. That
   gap — *the maker knew the product was defective and sold it anyway* — is the leverage that
   works against Meridian, Axiom and Sloane alike, and it is reachable from every lane because
   every lane can carry the ORACLE fact forward or reconstruct its equivalent. **The
   investigation stays solvable on every route** (autonomy law).

The endgame is **not** a kill-the-villain climax. It is Adrian, holding this, deciding **what to
do with a truth no institution will hold for them** — and the four lanes make that decision from
four different positions of evidence, allies, exposure and available exits.

## 2. Two owner decisions inside the truth

**Decision A — what happened to the prior Evelyn.** Recommended: **burned, then written off** —
she was compromised on the Singapore op, Meridian cut her loose, and rather than waste a proven
legend they inventoried it. (Keeps her a victim of the same machine, seeds a possible late
ally/ghost — a sister, a handler, a file — without a resurrection, and avoids a "she's secretly
alive and it's her on the board" twist that would cheapen the identity horror.) Alternatives:
*(b)* dead (cleanest, coldest); *(c)* retired and complicit (she sold her own legend — darkest,
but risks making her the reveal, competing with §3).

**Decision B — how completely Meridian resolves.** Recommended: **resolve the shape, name one
board signatory, leave the institution standing.** Adrian can wound Meridian (expose the
defect, extract terms, protect someone, walk with proof) but cannot topple a private
intelligence concern in one operation — and *shouldn't*, per "increased agency, not capture":
the win is that Adrian ends **holding leverage no one can take back**, not that the world is
fixed. A total-takedown ending would betray the noir and the autonomy frame.

## 3. The board-member reveal — the game's central turn (OWNER DECISION)

"One name on it you are almost sure you have met." This is the single most important unresolved
line in the game. Whoever it is, the reveal must (i) recontextualize an earlier meeting the
player already had, (ii) **not** punish a consent/intimacy choice (autonomy law — intimacy is
never revealed as a trap that the player is retroactively humiliated for), and (iii) be reachable
as a *position* from every lane, even if the vocabulary differs.

**Recommendation: Celeste Laurent.** She already knew the **prior Evelyn personally, perhaps
intimately**, in Singapore; she touched the current Evelynn's arm and said "You disappeared
before breakfast" (Ch6 canon, `mission.ts`). If Celeste sits on Meridian's board, every warm
thing she did becomes the coldest thing in the game: she did not mistake Adrian for an old
friend — she was **appraising the fit of the legend she helped sign away**. The horror is
personal and it lands on someone the player met as *warmth*, not as a patron they slept with.
It fulfills all three constraints cleanly, and it pays off the Ch6 decision to make Celeste the
personal corroborator over transactional Marcus.

**Alternatives (trade-offs named):**

| Candidate | Why it could work | Why it's weaker / the risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Celeste Laurent** *(rec.)* | Knew the original personally; her warmth becomes the reveal; punishes no intimacy choice | Requires her Ch6 corroborator role to stay firsthand-bounded until the endgame (already the rule) |
| **Julian** | Devastating for executive-lane players; ties the patron to the machine | **Violates autonomy law** — makes a consent/romance choice retroactively a trap; only some players met him closely; hard to reach from lanes that never engaged Julian |
| **Lena Voss** (Axiom Adaptive Medicine) | Clinical maker of the identity; thematically the "how" made flesh | Met as procedure, not as a person — weaker "did not expect"; risks making the clinic the villain, which the GDD guards against |
| **Marcus Chen** | Already a Ch6 witness; corporate reach is plausible | Transactional, impersonal — the reveal is information, not a wound; the map already made him the *fallback* witness |

**If the owner wants the reveal to scale with the lane** (an option, not a requirement): keep
**Celeste as the canonical signatory for all lanes**, but let each lane *reach* her differently —
executive players get there through a Helix-Meridian counterparty document, outside players
through Rook, institutional players through Sloane's cover, own-power players through the public
veil-peel. Same name, four roads. This preserves one canonical truth (route map decision 5) while
honoring "each lane reaches the truth by a different road."

## 4. Sloane's motive resolves here

Deferred from Chapter 6 by design. The resolution, playable (not a slide): **Sloane is a client's
officer who was handed a product her vendor's own system had already scored as uncontrollable.**
Her motive was never simple ownership — it was **survival inside Meridian's contract**: deliver a
usable asset from a legend she knew might slip, or be the officer who failed. Her Ch8 offer of
"cover" (to `own.exposed` players) reads, in hindsight, as either the one true thing she said or
the most useful lie — and the endgame **lets the player resolve which**, differently by lane:

- **Institutional lane:** Sloane can become a **bounded ally against Meridian** — she has the most
  to gain from exposing the defect that framed her, and the player can hold the ORACLE fact over
  her to force it. (Her exit vocabulary: an officer turning on her vendor.)
- **Outside lane:** Sloane is a **target or a trade** — Rook's material can burn her, and the
  player chooses whether her fall is useful or just.
- **Executive lane:** Sloane is a **rival inside overlapping client structures** — Julian/Helix and
  Axiom both answer to Meridian, and the player plays them against each other.
- **Own-power lane:** Sloane is the **door not taken** (or taken once, at a standing cost, if
  `own.crossover = institutional`) — the player resolves her from the outside, holding leverage
  she can't revoke.

In every case Sloane ends as a **person caught in the same machine**, not a defeated boss — the
noir keeps its moral ambiguity (GDD).

## 5. Reconvergence architecture — what the endgame reads (the entry contract)

Per route-map doctrine: **reconverge logistics, not biography.** The same operation reacts to who
arrived. The endgame reads a **bounded, sourced entry-state** and never a hidden score. Fields it
consumes (all already produced or trivially derivable — EVE Code owns exact plumbing):

| What the endgame reads | Source fields (examples) | What it changes in the operation |
|---|---|---|
| **The lane** | `route.lane`, `route.entry` | Which road to the board member is open; relationship vocabulary; which exit exists |
| **Crossover taken** | `own.crossover`, adjacent-lane flags | Whether a borrowed door is remembered (standing cost), who can be called |
| **Evidence in custody** | ledger leaf custody, `c8.meridian`, current-op evidence, `c7.finding` | How strong the case Adrian can make is; which claims are sourced vs inferred |
| **ORACLE fact held** | `c6.oracle-seen`, `c6.rook-proof` | Whether the controllability-defect lever is available directly or must be reconstructed |
| **Which witnesses will speak** | `c6` corroborator (Celeste/Marcus), Maya line, Sloane state | Who confirms the board signature; whose firsthand scope applies |
| **Public exposure** | `own.exposed`, `c5.published` | Whether Meridian already knows Adrian is coming; whether the public is a shield or a target painted on them |
| **Allies & debts** | `own.alliance.*` (rook/editor/maya), Julian state, Sloane offer | Who shows up; what it costs; who is spent already |
| **Resources & standing** | `own.cash`, provider arrangements | Which exits are affordable; whether Adrian can pay their own way out |
| **Overlay** | `route.overlay[]` (≤1, cause-driven) | A route-specific consequence vocabulary; never a different truth |

**Autonomy guard:** a player who reached the endgame with only the free-agent core (records-only
own-power, no allies, no ORACLE) must still be able to **name the board member and act** — via the
slow, sourced, public road. The truth is never gated behind a faction or an intimacy. The endgame
degrades *resolution and cost*, never *solvability*.

## 6. Chapter 9 — "Assembling the Case" (the bridge)

The convergent bridge: every lane arrives here, and here the player **assembles the case** from
what the route actually gave them. This is the diamond's right-hand join. Structure (mirrors the
hub/decision pattern, additive gated content revision, `VITE_EVE_CHAPTER9`):

- **Phase `arrive`** — a shared frame that reads `route.lane` + `own.crossover` for its entry
  prose ("You came to this holding X," told four ways), lands the Ch8 turn (Meridian = maker of
  people), and states the job: **you have the shape; now you assemble something you can act on,
  and you find the name.**
- **Phase `assemble`** — a **hub** of case-building moves, each gated by what the route holds and
  each contributing a *sourced* piece toward two outputs: **`case.strength`** (thin | supported |
  strong) and **`case.name`** (the board signatory, reachable on every lane by a different road,
  per §3). Moves (offered by state, hidden otherwise):
  - **assemble-witness** — take the corroborator (Celeste, or Marcus fallback) from firsthand
    Singapore scope to a confirmation that the signature *fits the person they knew*. Bounded
    exactly as Ch6 (§3–4 of the proof doc) — never pushed past real knowledge.
  - **assemble-oracle** — convert the held ORACLE fact into the controllability-defect lever
    (available directly if `c6.oracle-seen`; otherwise a reconstruction path at higher cost).
  - **assemble-evidence** — corroborate the ledger leaf / current-op evidence into a filing-grade
    chain (custody-dependent).
  - **assemble-ally** — spend a remaining ally (Rook debt, editor, Maya-bounded, or an
    institutional/executive contact if a crossover opened one) for the piece only they can give.
  - **assemble-name** — the road to the board signatory, one per lane, all reaching Celeste
    (own-power: public veil-peel; outside: Rook; institutional: Sloane's cover; executive:
    Helix-Meridian counterparty). Always reachable via at least the slow public road.
  - **assemble-stop** — move with what you have (autonomy: a thin case still names the edge).
- **Phase `resolve`** — computes `case.strength` and `case.name`, states plainly what Adrian can
  and cannot prove, and hands off to the endgame operation. Sets the endgame entry contract (§5).

Ch9 is the **last place biography accumulates**; the endgame operation (§7) then spends it.

## 7. The convergent endgame operation (Ch10+)

One operation, four positions. The player, holding an assembled case and a name, decides **what to
do with a truth no institution will hold**. Not a single verdict — a **position**. Phase sketch:

- **`approach`** — the operation reacts to who arrived (reads §5). Meridian's response scales with
  `own.exposed` (they see you coming or they don't); the board member (Celeste) is reachable for a
  confrontation whose vocabulary is the lane's.
- **`confront`** — the personal turn: Adrian and the board member who signed away the woman whose
  face they wear. Sloane's motive resolves in parallel (§4), her role set by lane. The scene's
  *content* is shared (the truth is one truth); its *leverage and options* are route-specific.
- **`position`** — the ending as a **held position**, built from route-specific exits the other
  lanes lack:
  - **Institutional:** force terms from inside; Sloane as bounded ally; Adrian keeps institutional
    protection but is known.
  - **Outside:** expose or trade the defect; truth others can't get, provenance risk owned.
  - **Executive:** enforce a term against Meridian through Helix/Axiom leverage; exit with rights
    intact and status kept or spent.
  - **Own-power:** hold the proof no one can revoke; audience as shield; Adrian ends beholden to
    no door — the template ending, and the proof the game is solvable alone.
  - **Overlay** (≤1): a route-specific consequence vocabulary layered on the above, cause-driven.
- Every position is consistent with **"increased agency, not capture."** No ending strands Adrian
  with less than they came in holding; the differences are *what kind of power* and *at what cost*.

## 8. Doctrine this endgame must not violate

- Reconverge **plot logistics, not biography** — the operation never rewrites what the player did,
  who they slept with, what they refused, or what it cost.
- **One canonical truth** (route-map decision 5): one board signatory, one Meridian shape, one
  Sloane motive — reached by four roads, not four different truths.
- **Autonomy law:** the name and the action are reachable on every lane, including the free-agent
  core alone. The endgame scales resolution and cost, never solvability.
- **No intimacy-as-trap:** the board reveal must not retroactively punish a consent choice (a
  primary reason to recommend Celeste over Julian).
- **Noir ambiguity intact:** Sloane and Celeste end as people in the machine, not defeated bosses;
  Meridian stands; the win is leverage held, not the world fixed.
