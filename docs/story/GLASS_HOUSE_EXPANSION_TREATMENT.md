# EVE — Glass House Expansion Treatment

- **Status:** Implemented story treatment; current runtime content version 11
- **Scope:** Expansion of the existing Glass House operation after the post-clinic apartment stop
- **Entry:** Post-transformation apartment departure
- **Exit:** Garage debrief and Rook reframe; no Chapter 3 continuation in this treatment
- **Target length:** Approximately 30–40 minutes for a normal engaged route

## Design goal

The existing operation remains the spine:

> enter as Evelynn → Marcus → Celeste → investigation → assessment → proof → extraction → Sloane / Rook

The expansion makes that spine feel like an operation rather than a sequence of fast text transitions. Evelynn’s appearance, credibility, social reading, restraint, evidence and information control become practical tools. A wrong or cautious judgment changes the predicament without ending the story.

The treatment preserves the uncertainty around the prior Evelynn, the unknown sender, Sloane’s larger motive and the meaning of the wafer. It does not add explicit sexual content, a new mission, a later transformation or a resolved conspiracy.

## Continuity and boundaries

The operation begins after **Home in Another Skin**. The car journey should not repeat the apartment’s mirror, wardrobe or Maya contact. It may refer to the selected presentation, one chosen accessory or one actual message.

The phone remains monitored, the invitation remains a Glass House credential and the restricted badge is not upgraded by presentation. Existing evidence custody is authoritative:

- audio is routed to Sloane’s recorder;
- a photograph remains on the monitored phone;
- a stolen token is a separate physical asset;
- possession or a photograph does not prove the wafer’s contents;
- a correct name can still be weakly reasoned.

NPC beliefs are attributed to what they observed or received. Private thoughts, historical records and another person’s claim do not merge automatically.

## Act 1 — Journey, arrival and presentation payoff

### Opening beat

Continue from the clinic vehicle. Sloane stays at Axiom and communicates through the earpiece. The city moves past the changed body: clothes catch on the seat, the reflection is unfamiliar, and ordinary traffic remains indifferent. The player can review Marcus’s brief, the invitation and existing Maya messages without creating new knowledge.

The driver completes the arrival, says goodbye and leaves Evelynn to cross the canopy into the Glass House. This physical handoff gives the player time to settle before the social operation begins.

### Admission

Reception verifies the invitation, compares the identity and watches the phone. The elevator journey gives Sloane one concise objective and one limit. The presentation alters who approaches Evelynn and what they assume:

| Presentation | Immediate opportunity | Immediate cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive | Staff accept a precise professional question and clear a purposeful route. | Institutional recognition and scrutiny increase; a wrong turn looks deliberate. |
| Socialite | Guests and Celeste’s circle make introductions easy. | More people remember where Evelynn looked and whom she left. |
| Shadow | Quiet edges and service traffic allow unobtrusive movement. | High-status openings are less available; initiating contact takes effort. |

Every presentation retains a viable route to every lead. The differences are in approach, attention and scrutiny, never in a hidden competence score.

### Preparation choice

Before the doors open, offer a short preparation action: review Marcus’s brief, examine exits, briefly mute Sloane, or study the reflection. Muting affects the earpiece only. It does not make the phone private, hide Evelynn from staff or cancel an already delivered instruction. The action is recorded as preparation, not identity preference.

## Act 2 — Marcus’s social duel

Marcus Chen approaches from the windows after leaving a group that expects his opinion. His appearance and position make his authority legible before he speaks. He greets Evelynn as the woman he remembers and references Singapore.

The four existing response shapes should become full exchanges:

1. **Ambiguity:** answer warmly without confirming a memory. Marcus supplies a detail and watches whether Evelynn adopts it.
2. **Challenge:** ask what he thinks he remembers. He gives a concrete anecdote, then turns the question back.
3. **Warmth:** accept the social rhythm while asking him to fill in the missing context. He becomes more attentive rather than fully reassured.
4. **An offered hand without an answer:** let the gesture carry the moment. Marcus interprets the silence as deliberate and tests the cover with a small follow-up.

Optional follow-up questions should append in selection order and appear immediately. Marcus may learn the exact words Evelynn says, her chosen name and visible hesitation. He cannot identify Adrian from a private thought or one awkward answer. Suspicion changes his attentiveness, not the operation’s availability.

The scene should create a useful ambiguity: Marcus may be remembering accurately, exaggerating, testing or manipulating. No tactic is universally correct.

## Act 3 — Celeste’s authenticity test

Celeste approaches from the central gathering. Her financial relationship with Marcus is stated, but her emotional recognition feels different from his controlled social test. She touches Evelynn’s arm, says the name and references a shared history.

Give the player four intentions:

- bluff a familiar response;
- redirect toward Marcus or the event;
- establish a boundary around the familiarity;
- ask what Celeste remembers.

Celeste can mention a Singapore restaurant, a person, an argument, jewellery or a previous opinion. The player may deliberately offer one incorrect detail. If Celeste corrects it, her source credibility becomes stronger; if she confidently accepts it, the player records a reason for caution. Her recollection remains an attributed claim, and her interpretation of what the past meant remains separate.

Marcus’s departure should be complete: he is called away, closes the immediate topic and leaves Celeste available for a later private approach. The operation does not force a romantic response or treat familiarity as consent.

## Act 4 — Investigation window

The room’s geography is clear: reception behind the quartet, central gathering, service corridor and gallery with the private table. Sloane states the attention limit before commitment. Use the existing opportunity model where possible: two deep investigations from a larger set, with an option to assess early or stop.

### Viable leads

| Lead | Possible finding | Cost / limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guest ledger | A concealed Halcyon credential authenticated through E. Benton. | Limited view; attention at reception and an access trace. |
| Service corridor | Camera interruption and a sighting placing Benton at the entrance. | Leaving the expected social position; no proof of transfer. |
| Marcus observation | His social pattern breaks when a blank place card appears. | Marcus may notice the scrutiny; it identifies a hidden contact, not a name. |
| Celeste follow-up | A Halcyon recollection and the “keep it narrow” connection. | Depends on source credibility and exposes curiosity; memory remains uncertain. |
| Security / authentication | An anomaly in how the private table or credential was cleared. | High scrutiny and institutional trace; do not grant unrestricted access. |
| Staff conversation | Hearsay about an unexplained arrival or service movement. | Weak, socially sourced evidence; it can create a trail without proving the allegation. |
| Restricted opportunity | Confirms that the invitation does not clear the private gallery; separate host authorization is required. | Significant exposure; the attendant refuses the roster, and no one is admitted. |

The implemented list preserves all seven leads above. The two-opportunity limit remains authoritative; an extra lead never grants the answer merely because the player selected it.

### Presentation-specific approaches

Executive lets Evelynn ask reception a precise question but invites authority questions. Socialite makes Celeste or another guest a natural route but leaves witnesses. Shadow permits a quiet corridor glance but does not make a staff door legal. All three can pursue all leads.

### Cost before commitment

Before each investigation, show the remaining opportunities, the expected visibility and what the lead can and cannot establish. Afterward show the result immediately, the source, its limits and the remaining opportunity. Rereading is free and does not improve evidence.

## Act 5 — Cover complication

After one investigation or the Marcus/Celeste exchanges, introduce one authored complication that forces a response without creating a new branch structure. Preferred options are:

- Marcus remembers a detail Evelynn cannot verify;
- Celeste asks a question the prior Evelyn should know;
- security notices an invitation inconsistency;
- a blank place card appears where Marcus expected a contact;
- a social obligation becomes operationally relevant.

The player can bluff, concede a partial truth, redirect, use presentation/status, sacrifice the remaining investigation opportunity, seek Celeste’s help or let Marcus believe a bounded falsehood. The exact response must state who hears it and what the player is risking.

The result records an observation and an attributed NPC belief. It does not create a universal lie flag, reveal Adrian or silently make Celeste an ally.

## Act 6 — Evidence convergence and source assessment

After the investigation window, Sloane asks for a source assessment. Preserve the options:

- Benton;
- Priya;
- Celeste;
- insufficient evidence.

Priya is known from Adrian’s prior context; a promotion is not evidence of involvement. Celeste’s recollection provides context, not direct placement. Marcus’s behaviour alone does not justify a name. Ledger or corridor evidence supports Benton without proving the purpose of the meeting.

Show a review before submission and permit revision. Keep reasoning quality separate from eventual accuracy. The authored result may be supported, weak, wrong or cautious. Do not reveal the correct answer through pre-commitment feedback or replace the selection.

## Act 7 — Proof acquisition

Before the exchange, explain the difference between proving the mission and owning the proof.

| Method | Immediate route | What is obtained | Ownership and limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audio | Route a live recording to Sloane. | Timely capture can preserve the substance of the exchange. | Sloane / Axiom owns the recording; Evelynn keeps no independent clean copy. |
| Photograph | Use the monitored phone at the right angle. | Timely frame can show both participants and the wafer transfer. | Evelynn retains the image on a monitored device; it cannot establish wafer contents. |
| Token theft | Approach Benton and take the separate access token. | A useful physical asset if the theft succeeds. | Evelynn possesses the token, but possession alone is incomplete proof of espionage and carries the highest exposure. |

The player commits only after seeing the method, cost and ownership. Late audio captures fragments, late photography establishes contact without the transfer and late theft fails to obtain the token. Do not call the photograph secret from Axiom or the token authenticated transaction evidence.

## Act 8 — Exchange and extraction

The exchange should be staged as three authored beats:

1. **Position:** Marcus moves toward the gallery and Benton’s presence becomes available or is delayed by the assessment.
2. **Transfer:** the wafer passes from Marcus to Benton; the separate token remains on Benton.
3. **Consequence:** the chosen method succeeds, captures a partial result or fails, and a person notices.

Naming Benton puts Evelynn in position even when the reasoning was weak. An incorrect or unresolved assessment costs the clean window and redirects attention; it does not end the story. The player should understand the causal link between the earlier judgment and the missed moment.

If the token is taken, resolve Benton’s grabbed wrist or confrontation before the escape begins. If it is not taken, do not narrate a second attempt. If audio or photography is late, make the missing part explicit.

Extraction varies by presentation:

- **Executive:** address the attendant or guard with controlled authority and request the lift. Scrutiny produces a name check and a report, not an invented detention.
- **Socialite:** join a departing group or use a social joke to make the elevator request ordinary. Witnesses remember the direction of travel.
- **Shadow:** follow service traffic without entering restricted space, then join guests at the lift. Lower visibility makes initiating the exit harder.

All routes reach the elevator. Suspicion changes the dialogue and delay, not the existence of an escape route.

## Act 9 — Sloane debrief

In the descending elevator, Sloane separates:

- the suspected source;
- what the evidence actually captures;
- what is missing;
- who controls each item;
- what the operation can responsibly claim.

She reveals that Benton was her probable source and that observing Evelynn’s independent judgment was another objective. Her account remains an attributed claim. Responses may accuse her, challenge the risk, recognize the test or withhold a reaction. They change the recorded exchange and her belief about the spoken response, not a hidden alignment meter.

Do not repeat every extraction explanation. The debrief should interpret the result at the level of mission responsibility and evidence custody.

## Act 10 — Rook reframe and ending

The unknown sender’s warning arrives in player-controlled steps. The interface and journal keep the sender unidentified. The three lines reframe Sloane’s debrief:

1. Benton was not the real test.
2. Evelynn was.
3. Sloane could have stopped the exchange.

Rook’s author-facing label remains hidden. The warning is an attributed claim, not proof that the sender knows the whole operation or that Sloane’s account is false. The player should understand the outcome first, then reconsider why Sloane permitted uncertainty.

Complete the elevator descent and physical arrival in the service garage. The ending summary lists assessment, evidence, ownership, exposure, missed opportunity and unresolved questions using earned records only. It offers journal/history review, save backup and confirmed restart. No Chapter 3 scene starts here in this treatment.

## State and information requirements for later implementation

The existing deterministic reducer and opportunity model should be extended only if a concrete scene needs it. Proposed records are:

- presentation, preparation action and visible scrutiny;
- Marcus and Celeste introductions, displayed replies and attributed interpretations;
- investigation leads, order, remaining opportunities and source limits;
- cover complication and who heard the response;
- assessment, reasoning quality and submitted name;
- capture method, timing, evidence quality and owner;
- object custody, wrist/confrontation state and extraction route;
- Sloane’s debrief response and the sender’s warning delivery.

Do not collapse these into a correctness score, identity preference or faction allegiance. A lucky correct guess remains weak reasoning. A useful token remains incomplete proof. A private thought remains private.

## Estimated duration and interaction density

For a normal engaged route:

- journey and admission: 4–6 minutes;
- Marcus social duel: 5–7 minutes;
- Celeste authenticity test: 5–7 minutes;
- investigation window and cover complication: 8–12 minutes;
- assessment and proof selection: 4–6 minutes;
- exchange and extraction: 5–8 minutes;
- debrief, warning and garage ending: 4–6 minutes.

This totals approximately 30–40 minutes, with optional rereading extending it. Meaningful activity is concentrated in social reading, source testing, opportunity tradeoffs, assessment, evidence ownership and extraction risk. Navigation and repeated document reading should remain short.

## Adult / dark tone

The Glass House already supports adult darkness through identity uncertainty, surveillance, social manipulation, exposure, dangerous recognition, power imbalance, temptation and leverage. No explicit sexual scene is required here. If a later authored handoff is discovered during implementation, it must be voluntary, pressured or coercive according to explicit state and never inferred from presentation, warmth or attraction.

## What would be padding

- repeating the clinic or apartment transformation explanation;
- adding more leads than the room can support;
- allowing every lead to be exhausted without attention cost;
- multiple versions of the same Marcus or Celeste question with no new source;
- a stat panel explaining presentation instead of showing its social effect;
- a capture method that changes wording but not custody or limits;
- a second debrief that repeats Sloane;
- a warning that explains the entire mystery;
- a forced romantic or sexual encounter;
- a combat or chase sequence that replaces the social extraction problem.

## Compatibility and versioning

Existing saves resume their original ledger without loading into the apartment automatically. The new story route uses the existing deterministic reducer, narrative history, sourced NPC observations, evidence records and two-opportunity investigation model.
